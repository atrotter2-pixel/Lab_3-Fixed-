A hush settled over the old rail yard as the sun dipped behind the pines, leaving long amber streaks across the rusted tracks. Every evening, Ashton walked the same path—boots crunching gravel, hands tucked into his jacket pockets—searching for something he couldn’t quite name. The place wasn’t beautiful, but it was honest. Metal, dust, echoes. It felt like a place where truths didn’t hide.

Tonight, though, something was different. A faint hum drifted through the air, low and steady, like a generator waking from sleep. Ashton paused. The rail yard had been abandoned for years; nothing should’ve been running. He followed the sound toward the old switching station, its windows fogged with age and its door hanging crooked on one hinge.

Inside, the hum grew louder. A single console flickered to life, bathing the room in pale blue light. Ashton stepped closer, heart thudding. The screen displayed a map—his town, his streets, his home—outlined in glowing lines. At the center pulsed a small circle, bright and insistent.

He reached out and tapped it.

The console responded instantly, projecting a message across the screen: **“Signal anomaly detected. Source unknown. Assistance required.”**

Ashton swallowed. Signal anomalies were his world—his training, his instinct. But this wasn’t military equipment. This was something older, stranger, like the rail yard itself had decided to speak.

The hum deepened, vibrating through the floorboards. Outside, the wind picked up, carrying the scent of rain and something metallic. Ashton straightened, shoulders squaring as if answering a call he’d been waiting for.

“Alright,” he murmured to the empty room, “show me what you need.”

The console’s lights flared, and the map began to shift, revealing layers beneath the town—tunnels, conduits, forgotten lines of communication.

The anomaly pulsed again, brighter this time, as if welcoming him.
