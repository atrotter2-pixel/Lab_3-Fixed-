A warm breeze rustled the pines around the old rail yard as the sun melted into a bright peach sky. Ashton took his usual evening stroll, kicking stray pebbles and hands shoved casually into his jacket pockets. He came out here mostly to avoid doing the dishes, enjoying the gentle clatter of rusty gravel under his sneakers.

Tonight, a cheerful, buzzy hum vibrated through the twilight—sounding suspiciously like a retro microwave warming up a burrito. Ashton paused, tilting his head. This place had been asleep for decades, yet the cheerful humming was definitely coming from the rickety wooden switching station.

He nudged open the crooked door.

Inside, an ancient computer terminal blinked to life with a triumphant *ding!*, casting a cozy neon glow across the dust motes. On the screen was a crisp map of his town, complete with a tiny, blinking green dot directly over the diner down the road.

Curious, Ashton reached out and pressed the screen.

A neon banner popped up: **"Emergency Alert: Coffee machine offline. Snacks depleted. Backup required."**

Ashton chuckled. "Alright, who set up a prank rig in the yard?"

The floorboards gave a playful rattle, and a second monitor flared awake with a high-pitched dial-up chime. Static danced across the glass before resolving into a remarkably familiar face.

It was Ashton. Or rather, Ashton from about fifteen years in the future, sporting messy salt-and-pepper hair and a vibrant Hawaiian shirt.

"Oh, thank goodness, it actually connected," the future Ashton sighed, adjusting a pair of crooked sunglasses. "Listen, kid. You've got exactly seventeen days."

Ashton blinked, leaning closer. "Seventeen days until what? Doomsday?"

"Worse," Future Ashton said gravely, leaning into the camera. "In seventeen days, Aunt Brenda tries to make her infamous tuna casserole for the block party. You have to intercept the groceries. You didn't stop it in my timeline, and my tastebuds have never recovered."

Ashton threw his hands up with a grin. "Wait, so I'm not here on some covert government mission?"
It was shockingly Ashton.

"Nah, you're the family's only hope," the older man winked, raising a coffee mug in salute. "Get to work, hero."

The screen blinked out with a cheerful pop, and the streetlights along the tracks flickered on like a runway, leading Ashton toward home.
“About time you found this place,” the man said. “You have exactly seventeen days before the signal wakes up.”

Ashton questioned.

“What signal?”

The older Ashton glanced off-screen, as if something was approaching. The image crackled.

“The one buried beneath the town. The one I failed to stop.”

The screen distorted for a moment before stabilizing.

“One more thing,” the older Ashton said quietly. “You aren't the one tracking the anomaly.”

He looked directly into Ashton’s eyes.

“You are the anomaly.”

The console went dark. Outside, every light in town suddenly flickered at once.
A shadowy mist materialized into a human-like being.
